*   Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Transformer layers.
*   Mechanism: How tokens are routed to experts.
*   Efficiency: Why it saves compute.
*   Hardware constraints: Problems when serving on a laptop GPU.

    *   *Concept:* Instead of one giant Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layer, we have many smaller "expert" FFNs.
    *   *The Router (Gating Network):* A learnable linear layer that takes the input token embedding and outputs a probability distribution over experts.
    *   *The Process:*
        1.  Input $x$ enters the router.
        2.  Router calculates scores $W_g \cdot x$.
        3.  Apply Softmax (often with "Top-k" selection, e.g., Top-1 or Top-2).
        4.  The selected experts process the token.
        5.  The outputs are weighted by the router scores and summed.
    s*   *Key term:* Sparsity.

    *   *The "Sparse" vs. "Dense" distinction:* In a dense model, every parameter is used for every token. In MoE, only a fraction of parameters are used.
    *   *FLOPs vs. Parameters:*
        *   Total parameters = Sum of all experts + router.
        *   Active parameters (per token) = Router + selected experts.
    *   *The Math:* If you have 8 experts and use 2, you've effectively reduced the compute cost of the FFN part by roughly 4x (depending on the architecture), while keeping the capacity of a much larger model.
    *   *Scaling:* It allows for massive model capacity (knowledge storage) without a linear increase in inference latency/FLOPs.
